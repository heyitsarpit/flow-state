# Range

1. Describe the actions the work must enable, preserving its goal and constraints.
2. Find other fields that solve similar problems. Research concrete methods and tools,
   rather than adopting expert personas or vocabulary.
3. Select at most three useful techniques. Explain the connection, required inputs,
   transfer assumptions, and cost of applying each technique.
4. Propose an independent check or bounded experiment that could establish each technique's value.

Return techniques, sources, applicability limits, and the recommended next experiment.
Do not expand the agreed scope or run proposed experiments without authorization.
